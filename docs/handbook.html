Operator handbook

Running Qiti, start to competent

Qiti is a cash-on-delivery shop. A customer fills a form on a landing page, the order lands in Telegram within seconds, someone calls to confirm it, and a courier collects the money at the door. This handbook is everything that person needs to do the job — read it once end to end, then keep it open for the reference parts.

01

How the shop works

Four things happen, in this order, for every sale. Nothing else in this handbook makes sense until this does.

  1. The page takes the order. A customer on a landing page fills in name, phone, wilaya, commune, and confirms. No payment online — this is cash on delivery.
  2. Telegram gets a message. Within a second or two, the order appears in the shop's Telegram group as a card with buttons.
  3. Someone calls the customer. Confirming by phone before shipping is the single biggest thing that stops returns. The dashboard tracks these calls.
  4. A courier delivers and collects the cash. Days later you record what happened — delivered, or returned.

Telegram is where the shop is run. It's fast, it's on your phone, and it's where orders arrive. The dashboard is where the shop is seen — the call queue, the archive, the numbers, the products and pages. They read and write the same data, so an accept in one shows up in the other. Neither has its own private copy of anything.

The one number to remember

An accepted order is not money. Revenue counts a sale only after you mark it delivered. An order can be accepted, shipped, and refused at the door — that's a loss, not a sale, and the reports are built to tell you the difference.

02

Getting your access

Join the Telegram group — ask the owner for the invite

Every order, every stock alert, and the nightly report land here. The bot only answers inside this one group; a private chat with it does nothing.

Open the dashboard — /admin on the shop's domain

Log in with the password, then the 6-digit code the bot posts in the group. That's why group access comes first — no group, no login. The session lasts 7 days, then asks again.

Send /help in the group

The bot replies with every command it knows. Sending a command by itself, with no arguments, explains how to use it — that's the fastest way to check yourself.

Read part 3 and part 4 before touching anything

They're the job. Everything after them is reference you can look up while working.

03

The life of an order

Every order walks this path. The buttons live on the order's Telegram message, and the message rewrites itself after each tap — so the last version you see in the chat is always the current truth.

pending → ✅ قبول الطلب → accepted → 📦 وصلت → delivered — revenue
↳ ↩️ أُرجعت → returned — loss → 📥 استلمتُ الإرجاع → back in stock
pending → ❌ رفض الطلب → denied — reason required

What each step actually does

StepStockMoneyWatch out
Accept Drops immediately, by the ordered quantity Nothing yet Refused if stock is short — the order stays undecided instead of going out with goods you don't have
Deny Untouched Nothing The bot asks why. Reply to its message with a real reason — it goes into the reports
Delivered Already gone Counts as revenue, and profit is locked in at today's costs Only tap this when the courier confirms the customer paid
Returned Still gone — on purpose Records the return loss straight away This means the courier turned around. The goods are not back yet
Return received Goes back up No change Tap only with the item physically in your hands, which can be a week later
Why returned and received are two taps

The money is lost the moment the courier turns around, but the item isn't back on the shelf for days. One tap for each keeps both numbers honest: the report shows the loss today, and stock only rises when you can actually ship the thing again.

What an order looks like in Telegram

📞 أكّدتُ هاتفياً 📵 عيّطتُ — ما جاوبش ✅ قبول الطلب ❌ رفض الطلب 💬 واتساب

The phone number is plain text on purpose — Telegram makes it tappable, so you call straight from the message. Tap 📞 أكّدتُ هاتفياً after the customer confirms on the phone; it doesn't accept the order, it just records that the call happened, which is what later tells you whether confirming is worth the time.

04

Your day, hour by hour

Morning — clear the queue

Open Call queue in the dashboard and work top to bottom. The order of the list is the order of the work: customers who already said yes, then calls that are due, then orders that ran out of tries and need a decision instead of another call.

  • Call the number. Log what happened with the buttons on the row — never in your head.
  • Customer confirms → log Answered, then Accept.
  • No answer, busy, phone off → log it. The next attempt schedules itself and the row leaves your list until then.
  • "Call me after five" → Other outcome… and set the time they gave you.

Through the day — orders as they land

New orders appear in Telegram and in the queue at the same moment. Handle them from whichever is in your hand. Call first, then accept or deny; don't accept a batch and "call them later".

As couriers report back

Open the order's message in Telegram and tap 📦 وصلت or ↩️ أُرجعت. When a returned parcel physically reaches the shop, tap 📥 استلمتُ الإرجاع so stock goes back up. Use /state to see everything still in transit.

End of day

At midnight the bot posts the day's report by itself: orders, accepted, denied, delivered, returned, real revenue, estimated profit, the deny reasons, anything still waiting, unfinished forms and stock. You don't have to wait for it — /state gives you the live version any time. On Sunday night a weekly report follows.

A good day, in one line

Queue empty or scheduled, every call logged, every delivered order marked the day the courier reports it, and no order older than 24 hours sitting undecided.

05

The call queue

/admin#/queue. Everything waiting on a call or a decision, oldest first. This is the page you work from; the Orders page is the archive you look things up in.

The four states

StateMeansDo
Confirmed by phoneCustomer said yes on the callAccept it. This is the closest thing to money on the page
Needs a callNever called, or the scheduled time has passedCall, then log the outcome
Out of triesFive attempts, or a wrong numberDecide — accept and ship blind, or deny with the reason
ScheduledHas a future callback timeNothing. It comes back to the top by itself

The outcomes, and what each one schedules

OutcomeNext tryUse when
Answered — confirmedNo next tryThey confirmed the order on the phone
No answer45 min, then longer each timeRang out
Busy15 minEngaged — the phone is in their hand right now
Phone off3 h, then longerOff or unreachable
Asked me to call backThe time they gave youThey answered but can't talk
Wrong numberStops callingNot the customer, or a bad number

After five attempts an order stops returning to the top and becomes Out of tries. That ceiling exists so the customer who never answers doesn't push down orders that could still sell. Add a note when something matters for next time — "wants the blue one", "asked for Friday" — it stays with the order forever and shows up on the Orders page.

Rows tagged Unfinished order are people who typed their phone number on the page but never confirmed. They're worth calling — they were buying a minute ago — but there's no order to accept, so the only action is Drop from queue once you're done with them.

06

Telegram reference

Commands only work in the shop's group. Send any of them alone and the bot explains it.

Orders

/state Everything not closed yet: undecided orders, orders in transit, returns not back at the shop. Orders older than 24 h are flagged ⚠️, and the call count shows as 📵3.
/leads People who entered a phone number and never finished the form.

Stock

/stock Every product and variant with its quantity and its number. ⚠️ marks anything at or below its alert threshold.
/restock <number> <qty> Adds to what's there, after a delivery from your supplier. /restock 3 20 — twenty more of product #3
/setstock <number> <qty> Sets the exact number, for corrections after a count. /setstock 3 12 — product #3 is now exactly twelve

Money

/cost Shows the four numbers profit is calculated from.
/cost product|ads|returns|courier <amount> Changes one of them, no redeploy needed. /cost ads 350 — advertising cost per order is now 350 DZD

Customers

/block <phone> [reason] Flags a number. Their orders still arrive, but marked — you decide, the bot doesn't block silently. /block 0661445566 refused three times
/unblock <phone> Removes the flag.
/blocked The whole list.

Catalog

plain message Describe new stock in your own words and the bot reads it, shows you what it understood, and waits for your confirmation before writing anything. عندي 9 أطواق تتبّع، أضف المنتج والفئة · add Toji outfit 4500 da x10 cost 2200
/newproduct The same thing in a strict format, when you'd rather not rely on the bot guessing.
/newcategory · /categories Create a category, or list them all.

Dangerous

/clear Deletes every order and resets stock to zero. It asks twice. There is no undo and no backup — never run it unless the owner told you to, in writing.
07

Stock

One number per product, per variant. The dashboard, /stock, the accept button's check and /restock all read and write that same number — there is no second counter to reconcile.

Serials

Every product has a small number — #1, #2 — assigned once and never reused. It's shown beside the product in the dashboard and in /stock, and it's what you type in /restock. A product with variants gets 3.1, 3.2, one per size or colour.

Restocking the wrong item is the easiest mistake to make here, so check the number in /stock before typing it, and read the bot's reply — it names the product it just changed.

Low stock

When a variant drops to its alert threshold, the bot posts one warning — once, not on every order — and the dashboard shows a Low stock card. The warning re-arms after you restock above the threshold.

When accept is refused

If stock won't cover the order, the accept tap is rejected and the bot names what's short. The order stays undecided. Restock, then accept. Bundles need every item in them: a bundle of a collar ×2 and a cover ×1 can't be accepted if covers are out, however many collars you have.

08

The money

Revenue counts delivered orders only. Accepted and in transit is not revenue — it's stock that has left the building.

Profit

profit = order total − goods cost × qty − advertising − courier cost, and a returned order counts as a straight loss of the return-loss figure. All four inputs come from /cost, so you can change them from your phone.

Courier cost defaults to zero because in cash on delivery the customer pays the delivery fee — it never comes out of your margin. Set it only if the shop is paying for delivery itself.

Costs are frozen at the outcome

The moment you mark an order delivered or returned, today's costs are copied onto that order. Change a supplier price next month and last month's profit stays exactly what it was. This is why you should mark outcomes on the day they happen, not in a batch at month end.

What the delivery fee is not

The order total includes the delivery fee, and that money goes to the courier. It is excluded from revenue and profit everywhere. Don't add it back by hand in any calculation you do outside the system.

09

Customers

Unfinished orders

When someone types a valid phone number and then leaves without confirming, the shop keeps it as an unfinished order and tells you a couple of minutes later. That message updates itself as they fill in more fields, and turns into "✅ أكمل الطلب" if they come back and finish. Call these people — they were buying minutes ago. They expire on their own after 30 days.

History and flags

Each order message carries what the shop knows about that phone number: past deliveries, past denials, past returns. ⚠️ زبون له سوابق means the number has refused or returned before — call before shipping, don't refuse on sight. ✅ زبون موثوق is the opposite, and a good reason to accept quickly.

The block list

/block flags a number for good reason — a repeat refuser, someone abusive. Blocked numbers can still order and their orders still arrive; they're just marked so you see it before deciding. Nothing is blocked silently, because a silent block loses customers you never find out about.

10

Products and pages

A product is the thing you ship — price, cost, variants, stock. A campaign is a landing page that sells it. One product can have several pages pointed at it.

Adding a product

Fastest from Telegram: describe the delivery in plain language and confirm what the bot understood. Or in the dashboard: Products → New, then fill name, slug, price, unit cost, and any options like size or colour. Every variant gets its own stock row.

Building a page

Campaigns → New, pick the product, then Load starter sections for a working page you edit section by section. The live preview renders through exactly the same code as the real page, so what you see is what visitors get. The page is invisible until you Publish it; publishing needs no deploy — save and the next visitor has it.

The slug is the URL. Keep it short and stable — it goes in ads and on packaging, and changing it later breaks every link already printed.

Ask before you do these

Changing a price, publishing or unpublishing a page, deleting anything, or editing the original collar landing page. Prices are printed in ads; a page that vanishes is money already spent on traffic.

Deleting

A product with orders in its history can't be deleted, and shouldn't be — its old orders would lose their name in every report. Archive it instead: it leaves the store and keeps its numbers.

11

Rules that keep it honest

Rule 1 — log the call, always

An unlogged call is a call nobody else can see. "No answer, third try" has to be in the system, not in your memory, or the next person starts from zero.

Rule 2 — deny with a real reason

"Never answered after five tries" and "too expensive" are different problems with different fixes. "No" tells you nothing next month.

Rule 3 — mark outcomes the day they happen

Delivered and returned both freeze costs at the moment you tap. Batching a week of outcomes on Friday puts Friday's costs on Monday's sales.

Rule 4 — received means in your hands

Tapping 📥 استلمتُ الإرجاع before the parcel is physically back makes the shop think it can sell something that's still in a van.

Never

Run /clear. Accept an order the stock can't cover by restocking a number you didn't count. Edit the original collar landing page's price in the dashboard — that price is baked into the page, and changing one side makes the page show one number while the courier collects another.

When something looks broken

SymptomAlmost alwaysDo
Buttons do nothing, commands are ignored, but new orders still arrive The bot's webhook lost its registration Open /api/telegram-webhook?setup on the shop's domain. It re-registers and tells you what it set. Anything you typed while it was down is discarded — send it again
A quantity in Telegram doesn't match the dashboard You're looking at two different variants Check the serial in /stock against the number in the dashboard. Same number means same row
Login says the session expired Seven days passed Log in again — password, then the code in the group
An order you accepted doesn't appear in revenue It isn't delivered yet Nothing is wrong. Revenue waits for 📦 وصلت
The queue shows nothing but you know orders are waiting They're scheduled, not missing Switch the filter to the whole queue and check the Scheduled column

Anything not on this list, or anything involving money already collected — tell the owner before you touch it. A wrong guess in a live shop costs real orders.
